What breaks first when you're out for three weeks

Handoff · 27 Aug 2026 · 4 min read

Ask an owner what stops when they're away and you'll get an answer straight away. It's usually quoting. They've known it for years, they've made peace with it, and they schedule around it.

The interesting part is the second answer, and the third. Those take longer to produce, and they're usually the ones that cost real money — because nobody has ever had to name them out loud.

The three-week test

A week away doesn't test anything. Most things can wait a week, and the ones that can't get handled by someone texting you once. Three weeks is where it starts to show: long enough that a quote goes stale, a customer calls twice, and a job ships without anyone invoicing it.

So the question isn't "could I take a holiday." It's narrower than that. Go down the list and answer yes or no:

  • A new inquiry gets answered the same day. Not eventually. That day.
  • A quote goes out, at the right price. Someone other than you can produce it and be right.
  • A customer asks what they paid last time. Someone can tell them without calling you.
  • Someone can say where a job stands. Without asking you, and without walking the floor to check.
  • Whoever called twice gets a call back. Because it's on a list, not because someone remembered.

Most owners check one or two. The gap isn't a character flaw or a hiring problem — it's that all five of those things currently run through one person, and that person built the company. It worked. It's still working. That's exactly why nobody's fixed it.

Everything you know that isn't written down is worth exactly nothing to whoever comes next.

Why it matters before you think it does

There's a version of this everyone's been pitched, usually by someone with an interest in the answer. Set it aside. It's the least likely of the three futures where this bites.

The other two are ordinary. Someone gets ill for six weeks and it can't wait. Or a son or daughter starts taking over and finds that the handover is mostly a series of phone calls to you, indefinitely, because the part that matters was never written down.

Those futures have one requirement in common, and it isn't a number. It's that the things only you know have to exist somewhere other than your head, so whoever comes next can pick them up. That's a smaller job than it sounds, and it doesn't start with software.

Where to start

Start with whichever line you couldn't check that costs you the most. For most shops that's quoting, because it's the one with a customer waiting on the other end of it. For some it's follow-up, because the inquiries are already arriving and nobody owns them.

Not all five at once. One process, written down, that somebody else can run — and then the next one. The point isn't to remove yourself from the business. It's that being away for three weeks stops being a thing you can't do.
