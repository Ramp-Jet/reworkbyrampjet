The readiness items that close in days.

For brokers, exit planners, CPAs and M&A counsel with a client whose website is a decade old and whose quoting lives in one person's head. We close those at a flat price and hand the acquirer documentation at close.

Why it's on your list

Everything else on the readiness list takes months: financials, contracts, concentration, management depth. The website can be finished this week, and it's the first thing a search fund or PE associate opens. Quoting and job tracking take a project, not a year. A 2011 site — and a business that stops when the owner is out — is a signal about the rest of the building.

What we tell your client

We don't say it raises the multiple.

It doesn't, and you'd hear about it if we did.

We say it's a cheap chip in two columns.

"Runs without the owner" and "documented systems" — plus it removes a first-impression risk with buyers who diligence from a laptop.

We say the footprint transfers on day one.

Nobody calls the seller about a domain renewal in month four.

How it runs

Day 1

One 30-minute call with the owner. We write the copy and source the photos; the client reviews instead of produces.

Day 2

Client marks up a link, we fix it, we switch the domain. Two revision rounds included.

Every quarter after

A one-page inspection: what's working, what's broken, what competitors changed. Included, ongoing, no clock.

At close

Transfer Packet plus one handoff call. If the deal takes three years or never closes, nothing changes for your client.
