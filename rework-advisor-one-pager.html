Rework for Advisors

The website line item,
closed in two days.

For brokers, exit planners, CPAs, and M&A counsel with a client whose site is a decade old. Rework rebuilds it at a flat price, holds it clean for however long the process takes, and hands the acquirer a documented Transfer Packet at close.

Why it's on your list

Everything else on the readiness list takes months: financials, contracts, concentration, management depth. The website can be finished this week, and it's the first thing a search fund or PE associate opens. A 2011 site is a signal about the rest of the building.

What we tell your client, and what we don't

  • We don't say a website raises the multiple. It doesn't, and you'd hear about it if we did.
  • We say it's a small, cheap chip in the "runs without the owner" and "documented systems" columns, and it removes a first-impression risk with buyers who diligence from a laptop.
  • We say the digital footprint will be transferable on day one, so nobody calls the seller about a domain renewal in month four.

How it runs

  • Day 1. One 45-minute call with the owner. We write the copy and source the photos; the client reviews instead of produces.
  • Day 2. Client marks up a link, we fix it, we switch the domain. Two revision rounds included.
  • Every quarter after. A one-page inspection: what's working, what's broken, what competitors changed. Included, ongoing, no clock.
  • At close. Transfer Packet to the acquirer, plus one handoff call. If the deal takes three years or never closes, nothing changes for your client.

Confidentiality

Nothing about a Rework signals a sale. To customers, employees, and the internet it's a company updating its site. We never reference the transaction, we don't tag the client anywhere, and we work with you, not around you.

How to hand it off

Suggested line to the client "Your website's on the list too. There's a group that rebuilds sites for companies like yours in two days for a flat $2,000, keeps an eye on it every quarter, and hands the buyer everything documented at close. One call, and it's off your desk. Want me to make the intro?"
